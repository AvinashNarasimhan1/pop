Lecturer in Digital Journalism → (Loyola Kaushal Kendra)

The Journey →

  • After stepping away from full-time employment to work as an independent journalist, I had been actively writing articles, series, and columns.
  • I was presented with an opportunity in 2017 to teach Digital Journalism to students in the newly established 'Kaushal Kendra' department at Loyola College. This proved to be not only a novel experience but also a deeply rewarding one.

The Process →

  • I truly enjoyed teaching students, engaging in discussions with them, and exchanging ideas and insights. It was a process of mutual learning. I also learned alongside with my students and for their benefit.
  • Having worked across various domains in journalism, with a particular focus on the internet and technology, teaching a subject rooted in digital media felt both natural and fitting.
  • The research I conducted for sourcing materials and reading texts to develop the curriculum further deepened my own understanding of digital technology and journalism.

Experience →

  • I served as an honorary lecturer in Digital Journalism for nearly seven years, with about four of those years functioning effectively as a full-time role. In the year 2020 of the COVID-19 pandemic, I took on a full-time teaching position conducted entirely online, which continued until 2024.

After Loyola →

  • As teaching began to take precedence, my writing work shifted to a part-time endeavor.
  • I eventually stepped down from my lecturing position to refocus on my writing and the compilation of my work into books.
  • Nevertheless, I find that I truly miss the students and the classroom environment.

→ (The following year, I had the opportunity to serve as a mentor and instructor for students specializing in Artificial Intelligence within a certificate program in Journalism, jointly conducted by Loyola College and the Government of Tamil Nadu.)

Takeaways →

  • Even though those are not full-time roles, I gladly embrace every opportunity to teach whether through lectures, workshops, or special events.
  • Whenever I introduce myself, I always take pride and pleasure in mentioning that I am an honorary lecturer.
  • My gratitude goes to the students who made me a teacher.
  • My experience at Loyola Kaushal Kendra opened doors for me to teach at several other colleges as well.
  • I remain to this day, always open to opportunities to teach and engage in discussions with students.

Wishes from Students →

  • Generally, I have never particularly expected or felt especially excited by birthday wishes. However, during my time as a lecturer in Digital Journalism, receiving wishes from students on Teachers' Day brought me a level of joy I had never experienced before. This affection from my students has been a deeply moving experience. It is truly memorable that, even years later, some of my former students still remember me and send greetings on Teachers' Day.
  • When I visit colleges for various events and happen to run into former students—now pursuing higher studies or working in professional environments—the joy I feel when they recognize me and call out, "That’s our 'Sir'!" is simply incomparable.

→ Thank you to all my students.

The Wonder of New Media →

  • I feel that my experience as a college lecturer has transformed me into a researcher of Digital Journalism.
  • Although I was already quite familiar with internet, digital-related technologies and trends, the course on 'New Media Studies' my first teaching assignment in the field of media proved to be a major eye-opener.
  • The books I sought out and studied to prepare for this course deepened my understanding of 'new media' and intensified my own inquiry into the subject. Given that there is no definitive, final definition for what constitutes 'new media,' I find myself still searching for the answer to that very question.
  • Modern perspectives on new media have further enriched my grasp of the internet and digital technologies, while also broadening my approach to reading beyond the purely technical aspects.

A Handbook on Mobile Journalism →

I have always held a fascination and respect for mobile phones particularly regarding their profound social impact. In this context, the opportunity to teach 'Mobile Journalism' as part of a digital journalism curriculum served as another significant eye-opener.

While researching and curating material on mobile journalism for my students, I realized that this is emerging as a major new trend regarding this field. Inspired by the pioneering initiatives and case studies I encountered, I authored a guidebook on mobile journalism.

Teaching this subject helped me forge a closer connection with my students and in turn helped them to connect more deeply with the subject.

Similarly, the opportunity to teach 'Convergent Journalism' a field where various media disciplines intersect offered a incomparable experience. I am currently considering writing a guidebook on this subject as well.
